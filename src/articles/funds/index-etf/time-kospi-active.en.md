<p class="lead">The fund uses the Korean equity market as its benchmark while its managers choose holdings and weights. An individual KOSPI company can therefore occupy a different share of the portfolio than of the benchmark.</p>

<section><h2>The underlying exposure</h2>
<p>The Korea Exchange’s KOSPI provides the comparison. The prospectus describes a reference split of 50% benchmark exposure and 50% active selection. This is a portfolio design rather than a guarantee of daily weights.</p>
<p>The manager&#x27;s published basket included <a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/samsung-electronics/">Samsung Electronics</a> at 26.05%, <a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/sk-hynix/">SK hynix</a> at 20.85% and Samsung SDI at 3.74%. 2026-11-25 · <a href="https://timeetf.co.kr/m11_view.php?idx=11&amp;cate=002">Composition source</a></p></section>

<section><h2>What the product holds</h2>
<p>The portfolio holds domestic equities and publishes a holdings list. Comparing it with the index on the same date separates the decision to include a company from the decision about how much to own.</p></section>

<section><h2>How it behaves</h2>
<p>The manager adjusts holdings subject to the benchmark correlation requirement. Two funds using the KOSPI can move differently because their active positions differ.</p></section>

<section><h2>How related products differ</h2>
<p><a href="/en/blog/funds/index-etf/time-us-nasdaq100-active/">TIME US Nasdaq 100 Active</a> is also active, but compares its portfolio with the Nasdaq-100. This fund starts with the Korean KOSPI.</p></section>

<section><h2>Where to look in the documents</h2>
<p>In Part II of the Korean prospectus, the objective and strategy identify the benchmark and permitted assets. Investment risks covers tracking and applicable currency exposure. Check both the stated fee and total expense figures, then the trust agreement for limits and payment terms.</p></section>
