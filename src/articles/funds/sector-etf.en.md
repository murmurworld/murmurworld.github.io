<section><h2>How a sector becomes an investable list</h2>
<p><a href="/en/blog/funds/sector-etf/xle/">XLE</a> starts with energy companies already in the S&amp;P 500. <a href="/en/blog/funds/sector-etf/kodex-banks/">KODEX Banks</a> tracks the Korea Exchange&#x27;s bank index. Even the same replication method gives different holdings when eligibility changes by listing market, size or business.</p>
<p>Miners inside <a href="/en/blog/funds/sector-etf/copx/">COPX</a> sell copper but also pay for fuel, equipment and labor. <a href="/en/blog/funds/gold-commodities/cper/">CPER</a> obtains copper exposure through futures contracts and rolls. That changes what a supply interruption, higher mining costs or a steep futures curve means for each product.</p></section>

<section><h2>Why do SOXX and SMH hold different chip companies?</h2>
<div class="pmap"><div class="co side"><p>SOXX uses ICE’s NYSE Semiconductor Index, a modified capitalization-weighted index of 30 U.S.-listed semiconductor companies. <a href="/en/blog/funds/leveraged/soxl/">SOXL</a> targets three times its daily change. SMH uses the MVIS US Listed Semiconductor 25 Index, selecting 25 U.S.-listed semiconductor and equipment companies under its own eligibility and weighting rules. Different eligibility and constituent counts produce different holdings.</p><p><a href="https://www.ishares.com/us/products/239705/ishares-phlx-semiconductor-etf">iShares SOXX</a> · <a href="https://www.vaneck.com/us/en/investments/semiconductor-etf-smh/overview/">VanEck SMH</a></p></div></div></section>
