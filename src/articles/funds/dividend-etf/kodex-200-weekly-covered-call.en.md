<section><h2>The underlying exposure</h2>
<p>The KRX index combines an equity basket with weekly call writing. The 15% figure in the index name is a premium target used to size the overlay. The proportion of calls sold can vary.</p>
<p>The creation basket included <a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/samsung-electronics/">Samsung Electronics</a> at 27.96%, <a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/sk-hynix/">SK hynix</a> at 19.91% and KODEX 200 ETF at 14.60%. <section><h2>What the product holds</h2>
<p>The call buyer has a contractual claim on gains above the strike. The fund receives the premium and owes the payoff on the written portion when the option settles in the money.</p></section>

<section><h2>How it behaves</h2>
<p>Premiums can absorb part of an equity decline, while larger losses remain possible. A strong rise can exceed the upside retained on the covered portion. Distribution funding and the cash actually paid are separate items.</p></section>

<section><h2>How related products differ</h2>
<p><a href="/en/blog/funds/dividend-etf/schd/">SCHD</a> selects dividend-paying shares. KODEX also receives option premiums, giving up upside above the strike on the covered portion.</p></section>

<section><h2>Where to look in the documents</h2>
<p>In Part II of the Korean prospectus, the objective and strategy identify the benchmark and permitted assets. Investment risks covers tracking and applicable currency exposure. Check both the stated fee and total expense figures, then the trust agreement for limits and payment terms.</p></section>
