<p class="lead">DGRO looks at the history of dividend increases and the share of earnings being paid out. Its index weights constituents by the dividends paid rather than simply by company size.</p>

<section><h2>The underlying exposure</h2>
<p>Morningstar removes the highest-yielding decile before applying at least five years of dividend growth and an earnings payout ratio below 75%, among other tests. Dividend-dollar weights are capped at 3% per stock at rebalancing.</p>
<p>Fund weights included <a href="/en/blog/companies/technology/microsoft/">Microsoft</a> at 3.22%, <a href="/en/blog/companies/technology/apple/">Apple</a> at 3.16% and <a href="/en/blog/companies/industry-energy-materials/exxonmobil/">ExxonMobil</a> at 3.11%. 2026-09-30 · <a href="https://www.ishares.com/us/literature/fact-sheet/dgro-ishares-core-dividend-growth-etf-fund-fact-sheet-en-us.pdf">Composition source</a></p></section>

<section><h2>What the product holds</h2>
<p>Representative sampling lets the fund hold stocks that approximate the index’s characteristics. The timing of company dividends, fund expenses and shareholder distributions can differ.</p></section>

<section><h2>How it behaves</h2>
<p>The payout test can exclude an otherwise established dividend payer. Another dividend fund can hold a similar number of stocks while carrying quite different sector and company exposures.</p></section>

<section><h2>How related products differ</h2>
<p><a href="/en/blog/funds/dividend-etf/vig/">VIG</a> screens for a decade of dividend increases. DGRO&#x27;s benchmark uses five years of growth and payout criteria, then applies a different weighting method.</p></section>

<section><h2>Where to look in the documents</h2>
<p>Investment Objective defines the target. Fees and Expenses identifies annual costs and any waivers. Principal Investment Strategies explains the benchmark and instruments. Read Principal Risks alongside the holdings to identify the source of each exposure.</p></section>
