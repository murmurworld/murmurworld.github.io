<section><h2>The underlying exposure</h2>
<p>The S&amp;P U.S. Dividend Growers Index generally requires ten consecutive years of higher annual dividends. It screens out part of the highest-yielding universe and weights eligible stocks by market value.</p>
<p>Fund weights included <a href="/en/blog/companies/technology/microsoft/">Microsoft</a> at 4.9%, <a href="/en/blog/companies/technology/apple/">Apple</a> at 4.9% and <a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/broadcom/">Broadcom</a> at 4.3%. 2026-09-30 · <a href="https://fund-docs.vanguard.com/F0920.pdf">Composition source</a></p></section>

<section><h2>What the product holds</h2>
<p>Vanguard holds constituent stocks using full replication. The largest portfolio exposures follow index weights, so a ranking by cash dividend alone does not identify the stocks that matter most.</p></section>

<section><h2>How it behaves</h2>
<p>A company that breaks its growth record can lose eligibility at review. The index measures dividend history, while each company continues to decide its future payments.</p></section>

<section><h2>How related products differ</h2>
<p><a href="/en/blog/funds/dividend-etf/dgro/">DGRO</a> also screens for dividend growth. VIG looks for ten consecutive annual increases, while DGRO&#x27;s index uses a five-year growth screen and a payout test.</p></section>

<section><h2>Where to look in the documents</h2>
<p>Investment Objective defines the target. Fees and Expenses identifies annual costs and any waivers. Principal Investment Strategies explains the benchmark and instruments. Read Principal Risks alongside the holdings to identify the source of each exposure.</p></section>
