<p class="lead">KORU trades in U.S. dollars and targets three times the daily move in an index of large and mid-sized Korean companies. Those companies trade on the Korean market, so currency translation and the gap between the two market closes both matter.</p>

<section><h2>The underlying exposure</h2>
<p>MSCI Korea 25/50 covers roughly 85% of Korea’s float-adjusted market capitalization. At rebalancing, one issuer is capped at 25% and issuers above 5% together at 50%. Weights drift between reviews.</p>
<p>The benchmark contained <a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/sk-hynix/">SK hynix</a> at 25.43% and <a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/samsung-electronics/">Samsung Electronics</a> at 23.25%, a snapshot of weights between rebalances. 2026-09-30 · <a href="https://www.direxion.com/product/daily-msci-south-korea-bull-3x-etf">Composition source</a></p></section>

<section><h2>What the product holds</h2>
<p>The fund can use swaps, futures, index securities and an underlying index ETF. Both Korean share values and currency translation into U.S. dollars feed into the exposure.</p></section>

<section><h2>How it behaves</h2>
<p>Korean and U.S. markets close at different times. Currency moves after the Korean close, concentrated company weights and daily compounding can all matter to the U.S.-listed share.</p></section>

<section><h2>How related products differ</h2>
<p><a href="/en/blog/funds/leveraged/soxl/">SOXL</a> also resets daily to a triple target. KORU tracks a country benchmark across industries, while SOXL tracks a U.S.-listed semiconductor industry benchmark.</p></section>

<section><h2>Where to look in the documents</h2>
<p>Investment Objective defines the target. Fees and Expenses identifies annual costs and any waivers. Principal Investment Strategies explains the benchmark and instruments. Read Principal Risks alongside the holdings to identify the source of each exposure.</p></section>
