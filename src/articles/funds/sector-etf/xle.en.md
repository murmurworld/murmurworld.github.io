<p class="lead">XLE carves the energy sector out of the S&amp;P 500. The fund owns businesses that produce or service energy rather than contracts for barrels of fuel.</p>

<section><h2>The underlying exposure</h2>
<p>The Energy Select Sector Index screens S&amp;P 500 companies for oil, gas and consumable fuels or energy equipment and services. Modified market-cap weighting gives larger producers a prominent role. At quarterly updates, S&amp;P DJI refreshes share counts and reapplies its concentration rules.</p>
<p>Fund weights included <a href="/en/blog/companies/industry-energy-materials/exxonmobil/">ExxonMobil</a> at 23.86%, <a href="/en/blog/companies/industry-energy-materials/chevron/">Chevron</a> at 18.84% and <a href="/en/blog/companies/industry-energy-materials/conocophillips/">ConocoPhillips</a> at 7.39%. 2026-11-24 · <a href="https://www.ssga.com/us/en/intermediary/etfs/state-street-energy-select-sector-spdr-etf-xle">Composition source</a></p></section>

<section><h2>What the product holds</h2>
<p>The portfolio holds equities. A producer that sells oil and a service company paid to operate drilling equipment can earn quite different margins in the same oil market.</p></section>

<section><h2>How it behaves</h2>
<p>A change in sector classification or S&amp;P 500 membership can change eligibility. Concentration also makes company-specific events such as disruption at a large producer significant for the portfolio.</p></section>

<section><h2>How related products differ</h2>
<p><a href="/en/blog/funds/sector-etf/copx/">COPX</a> also owns resource businesses. XLE takes the energy sector of the S&amp;P 500, while COPX selects copper miners around the world.</p></section>

<section><h2>Where to look in the documents</h2>
<p>Investment Objective defines the target. Fees and Expenses identifies annual costs and any waivers. Principal Investment Strategies explains the benchmark and instruments. Read Principal Risks alongside the holdings to identify the source of each exposure.</p></section>
