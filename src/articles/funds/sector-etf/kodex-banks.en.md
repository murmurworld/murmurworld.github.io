<section><h2>The underlying exposure</h2>
<p>The Korea Exchange’s KRX Banks Index uses bank industry classification and free-float market value. Periodic index changes can add or remove listed groups that control banking subsidiaries. The prospectus schedules the annual review for the trading day after the September KOSPI 200 futures expiry.</p>

<section><h2>What the product holds</h2>
<p>The basket lists individual holding-company shares and separate won cash. A bank holding company can own card or securities subsidiaries as well, so its consolidated business can extend beyond deposit taking.</p></section>

<section><h2>How it behaves</h2>
<p>Several banks can share exposure to the same loan market and credit cycle. ETF shares carry equity risk rather than the contractual return and deposit protections of a bank account.</p></section>

<section><h2>How related products differ</h2>
<p>Like <a href="/en/blog/funds/sector-etf/xle/">XLE</a>, this is a one-sector portfolio. Korean banks face loan spreads and credit costs, while U.S. energy companies face oil, gas and refining economics.</p></section>

Check both the stated fee and total expense figures, then the trust agreement for limits and payment terms.</p></section>
