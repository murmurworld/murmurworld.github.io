<p class="lead">A GLD share represents an interest in a trust holding gold bullion. The underlying asset is metal in custody, net of the trust’s expenses.</p>

<section><h2>The underlying exposure</h2>
<p>The trust values its bullion using the London afternoon gold benchmark under its valuation rules. The relevant details are ounces, fineness and valuation time rather than constituent selection by industry.</p>
<p>The year-end custody and asset schedules identify bullion held for the trust. The assets are gold bars, giving a different exposure from mining shares. 2025-09-30 · <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1222333/000143774925036305/gld20250930_10k.htm">Composition source</a></p></section>

<section><h2>What the product holds</h2>
<p>Gold is held through custodial arrangements. Authorized participants create or redeem large baskets against gold. Ordinary exchange trades transfer existing trust shares between market participants.</p></section>

<section><h2>How it behaves</h2>
<p>Bullion generates no interest. Sales of small amounts of gold to pay expenses can reduce ounces per share over time. The bullion inventory and the expense-related sale entries describe different parts of that process.</p></section>

<section><h2>How related products differ</h2>
<p><a href="/en/blog/funds/gold-commodities/iaum/">IAUM</a> is another U.S. bullion trust, with different sponsor fees and gold per share. <a href="/en/blog/funds/gold-commodities/ace-krx-gold/">ACE KRX Gold Spot</a> instead references Korea&#x27;s won-denominated spot market.</p></section>

<section><h2>Where to look in the documents</h2>
<p>Read the investment schedule for bullion. Item 1 Business of the annual report covers custody, creation and redemption. Item 1A Risk Factors covers custody and trading exposure. The Sponsor’s Fee note explains how expenses are funded.</p></section>
