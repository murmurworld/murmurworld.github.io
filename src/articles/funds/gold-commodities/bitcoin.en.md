<p class="lead">Bitcoin is the network’s native settlement unit. BTC-USD denotes bitcoin quoted in dollars. It does not identify a fund issued by a company.</p>

<section><h2>The underlying exposure</h2>
<p>Nodes check signatures and transaction rules. The proof-of-work chain records transactions and determines which outputs remain unspent. It is not an index of companies or bonds.</p>
<p>Section 2 of the white paper follows the signatures that transfer the right to spend from one owner to the next. 2008-10-31 · <a href="https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf">Composition source</a></p></section>

<section><h2>What the product holds</h2>
<p>With direct custody, wallet software manages the keys used to authorize a spend. Holding a balance with an intermediary places withdrawal and key-management functions with that intermediary.</p></section>

<section><h2>How it behaves</h2>
<p>The protocol has neither an annual fund expense ratio nor a maturity date. On-chain transaction fees pay for inclusion in a block. Loss of keys or a transfer to an unintended address can leave funds unrecoverable.</p></section>

<section><h2>How related products differ</h2>
<p><a href="/en/blog/funds/gold-commodities/gld/">GLD</a> represents an interest in gold held in custody. Direct bitcoin ownership is exercised through spendable outputs on the chain and the keys that authorize spending.</p></section>

<section><h2>Where to look in the documents</h2>
<p>Read sections 2, 4, 5 and 6 of the white paper for signatures, proof of work, node behavior and incentives. The developer guide connects these concepts to transaction outputs and block rules.</p></section>
