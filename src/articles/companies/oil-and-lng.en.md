Gasoline at a filling station and fuel for a gas-fired power plant are both products of energy companies. To understand what a company earns, first identify what its customers buy. A refinery chooses crude oil it can process. A gas importer looks for LNG cargoes it can receive at a particular terminal and time.

## Selling crude and making fuel

Oil and gas producers extract crude oil and natural gas. A refinery buys crude oil from producers or traders and makes products such as gasoline, diesel, and jet fuel. How heavy the crude is and how much sulfur it contains affect the equipment the refinery needs and the mix of products it can make. A refinery processing high-sulfur crude, for example, has to plan for sulfur removal when making fuels.

ExxonMobil does both jobs. Its 2025 Form 10-K covers oil and gas production in Upstream and refining and fuels in Energy Products. When crude prices move, look separately at what the production business receives and what the refining business pays for feedstock and receives for its products. [EIA, crude oil refining](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/oil-and-petroleum-products/refining-crude-oil-inputs-and-outputs.php) · [ExxonMobil 2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/34088/000003408826000045/xom-20251231.htm)

## How natural gas gets onto a ship

LNG is natural gas cooled until it becomes liquid. It takes up less space in that form, which makes it easier to ship. The sequence is **liquefaction → shipping → unloading and storage → regasification → pipeline delivery**. Once converted back into gas, it can travel by pipe to power stations and factories. [EIA, LNG explained](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/natural-gas/liquefied-natural-gas.php)

Cheniere sells LNG from U.S. facilities including Sabine Pass in Louisiana. In 2017, Korea Gas Corporation began receiving commercial supplies under a long-term Sabine Pass contract. POSCO International’s Gwangyang terminal illustrates what a receiving terminal does in South Korea. It stores LNG unloaded from ships and turns it back into gas for delivery to customers. [Cheniere’s KOGAS contract announcement](https://lngir.cheniere.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/163/cheniere-kogas-celebrate-commencement-of-20-year-lng) · [Gwangyang LNG terminal operations, Korean](https://newsroom.posco.com/kr/%ED%8F%AC%EC%8A%A4%EC%BD%94%EC%9D%B8%ED%84%B0%EB%82%B4%EC%85%94%EB%84%90-%EA%B4%91%EC%96%91-%EC%A0%9C1-lng%ED%84%B0%EB%AF%B8%EB%84%90-%EC%A2%85%ED%95%A9%EC%A4%80%EA%B3%B5/)

## Compare offers at the same destination

Suppose an importer considers offers from Cheniere and Shell. First match the cargo quantity, destination, and delivery window. Then check who arranges the ship and pays the freight under the two delivery terms in Cheniere’s filing.

- **FOB (Free on Board)** means the seller loads the LNG onto the buyer’s nominated ship at the loading port. The importer accounts separately for the onward voyage and freight.
- **DAP (Delivered at Place)** means the seller transports the cargo to the agreed place and makes it available for unloading. The importer checks the exact delivery point and unloading terms in the contract. [Cheniere 2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/3570/000000357026000005/lng-20251231.htm) · [International Chamber of Commerce on DAP](https://academy.iccwbo.org/incoterms/article/incoterms-2020-dap-or-ddp/)

In its 2025 filing, Cheniere says its long-term sales agreements generally combine a fixed fee with a variable fee linked to Henry Hub, a U.S. natural gas benchmark. Some contracts require buyers who cancel or suspend a cargo to pay the fixed fee on the contracted volume. Shell sells LNG from its own production and sources cargoes from others. The buyer can compare delivered offers from both companies while recognizing how each seller sources the LNG and earns its revenue. [Cheniere 2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/3570/000000357026000005/lng-20251231.htm) · [Shell 2025 annual report](https://www.shell.com/investors/results-and-reporting/annual-report.html)

Check what the contract’s benchmark measures. **Henry Hub** is a reference point for natural gas traded in the U.S. pipeline market. **JKM** assesses spot LNG cargoes delivered to Northeast Asia. The product and delivery basis help explain which costs a quoted price covers. [CME on Henry Hub](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/courses/introduction-to-energy/introduction-to-natural-gas/nat-gas-product-overview) · [S&P Global on the JKM assessment](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/pricing-benchmarks/assessments/lng/jkm-japan-korea-marker-gas-price-explained)

## Three places to look in the filings

- **ExxonMobil 2025 10-K, Note 3.** Check the scope of Upstream and Energy Products. Chemical Products and Specialty Products are separate reportable segments. [ExxonMobil’s segment disclosures](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/34088/000003408826000045/xom-20251231.htm)
- **Cheniere 2025 10-K, Liquidity and Capital Resources.** Read how fixed and variable fees work. Keep amounts due under future contracts separate from revenue recognized during the reporting period. [Cheniere’s contract and financial disclosures](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/3570/000000357026000005/lng-20251231.htm)
- **Shell 2025 annual report, Integrated Gas.** Compare LNG liquefaction and sales volumes. The sales figure can also reflect LNG purchased from third parties. [Shell’s Integrated Gas reporting](https://www.shell.com/investors/results-and-reporting/annual-report.html)
