<h2>Follow the money through one AI server</h2>

<p>A data center obtains an accelerator, often inside a server assembled by another company. NVIDIA names TSMC and Samsung as wafer foundries and SK hynix, Micron and Samsung as memory suppliers. It also says it uses CoWoS packaging. A computing die and nearby high-bandwidth memory can share a package.</p>

<p>Part of the chip bill covers fabrication, memory and packaging. Fabs in turn buy equipment from suppliers such as Lam Research and KLA. After the server arrives, its owner still has to power and cool it. Those continuing expenses are distinct from the chip purchase.</p>

<h2>The 16 companies in this sector</h2>

<div class="roster">

<div><h3>Chip designers</h3><ul>

<li><b><a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/nvidia/">NVIDIA</a><small>NVDA</small></b><span>Sells GPUs for training and running AI models, together with the networking needed to connect many accelerators. It also sells graphics products for games.</span></li>

<li><b><a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/amd/">AMD</a><small>AMD</small></b><span>Designs CPUs for servers and PCs, graphics processors and AI accelerators. External foundries manufacture the chips.</span></li>

<li><b><a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/broadcom/">Broadcom</a><small>AVGO</small></b><span>Designs data-center switching chips and custom accelerators for large customers. Its infrastructure software business includes VMware.</span></li>

<li><b><a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/qualcomm/">Qualcomm</a><small>QCOM</small></b><span>Sells application processors and cellular modems to device makers. A separate licensing business earns royalties on wireless patents.</span></li>

</ul></div>

<div><h3>Foundries, memory makers and integrated manufacturers</h3><ul>

<li><b><a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/tsmc/">TSMC</a><small>TSM</small></b><span>Produces and packages chips designed by customers. A foundry earns manufacturing revenue when those customers order production.</span></li>

<li><b><a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/samsung-electronics/">Samsung Electronics</a><small>005930</small></b><span>Manufactures DRAM and NAND, offers foundry services and sells finished products including phones, TVs and appliances.</span></li>

<li><b><a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/sk-hynix/">SK hynix</a><small>000660</small></b><span>Makes working memory for servers and devices, stacked high-bandwidth memory used with accelerators and NAND flash storage.</span></li>

<li><b><a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/micron/">Micron</a><small>MU</small></b><span>Sells DRAM, HBM and NAND, including solid-state drives built from NAND for data centers and other customers.</span></li>

<li><b><a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/sandisk/">Sandisk</a><small>SNDK</small></b><span>Turns flash memory into data-center SSDs, embedded storage and removable products such as memory cards.</span></li>

<li><b><a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/intel/">Intel</a><small>INTC</small></b><span>Designs PC and server CPUs and runs wafer factories. Intel Foundry also offers production services to external customers.</span></li>

<li><b><a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/texas-instruments/">Texas Instruments</a><small>TXN</small></b><span>Its analog chips manage power or read signals. Its embedded processors control functions in industrial equipment, vehicles and other devices.</span></li>

<li><b><a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/analog-devices/">Analog Devices</a><small>ADI</small></b><span>Makes chips that convert physical measurements to digital information, and devices that manage power in factories, vehicles and communications equipment.</span></li>

</ul></div>

<div><h3>Fab equipment makers</h3><ul>

<li><b><a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/asml/">ASML</a><small>ASML</small></b><span>Supplies machines that project circuit patterns onto silicon wafers, along with upgrades and service for installed systems.</span></li>

<li><b><a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/applied-materials/">Applied Materials</a><small>AMAT</small></b><span>Makes and services equipment that deposits, removes, modifies and inspects layers on a wafer as chips are fabricated.</span></li>

<li><b><a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/lam-research/">Lam Research</a><small>LRCX</small></b><span>Supplies wafer-processing tools that remove material or add precisely controlled films, and supports tools already in fabs.</span></li>

<li><b><a href="/en/blog/companies/semiconductors/kla/">KLA</a><small>KLAC</small></b><span>Supplies tools that find wafer defects and measure features during production so a fab can detect process problems.</span></li>

</ul></div>
