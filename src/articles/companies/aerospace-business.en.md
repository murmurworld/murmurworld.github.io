Two airlines can fly the same Airbus A320neo with different engines. One chooses the LEAP and the other chooses the GTF. Both have to arrange maintenance once the aircraft enter service. In space, customers may pay a company to launch a satellite or subscribe to internet service using a satellite network. Who buys what is a good place to start when reading an aerospace company’s accounts.

## Two engines compete on the same airframe

Airbus offers the A320neo with **CFM International’s LEAP‑1A** or **Pratt & Whitney’s PW1100G‑JM**, a member of the GTF family. Airbus makes the airframe. CFM is a joint venture between GE Aerospace and Safran. Pratt & Whitney is a business segment of RTX. When airlines choose between the two engines, they also have to plan for spares, replacement engines and maintenance. [Airbus’s A320neo engine options](https://build.airbus.com/2-template-a320neo/) · [GE Aerospace FY2025 Form 10‑K](https://www.geaerospace.com/sites/default/files/geaerospace_10k_01292026.pdf) · [RTX FY2025 Form 10‑K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/101829/000010182926000006/rtx-20251231.htm)

## Maintenance carries on after delivery

As airlines fly their engines, they buy spare parts and repairs and send engines to a shop for major work called an **overhaul**. The industry groups maintenance, repair and overhaul under **MRO**. Some services are purchased job by job. With a contract such as Rolls-Royce’s TotalCare, the airline pays according to flying hours. [Pratt & Whitney’s maintenance services](https://www.rtx.com/en/prattwhitney/services/enginewise/maintenance) · [Rolls-Royce’s TotalCare contract](https://www.rolls-royce.com/products-and-services/civil-aerospace/services/totalcare)

In fiscal 2025, services accounted for about **75%** of GE Aerospace’s **Commercial Engines & Services** revenue. That figure covers the whole segment. To follow the engines already in use, look at shop visits, parts sales and long-term service agreements alongside deliveries of new engines. [GE Aerospace FY2025 Form 10‑K](https://www.geaerospace.com/sites/default/files/geaerospace_10k_01292026.pdf)

## Governments buy through procurement contracts

Pratt & Whitney lists government customers alongside aircraft manufacturers and airlines. A government can contract separately for development, equipment deliveries or support over the life of the equipment. U.S. federal rules distinguish **fixed-price** contracts from **cost-reimbursement** contracts, under which the government pays allowable costs. An announced award, the contract work still to be performed and the revenue recognized in a reporting period are three figures worth looking up separately. [RTX FY2025 Form 10‑K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/101829/000010182926000006/rtx-20251231.htm) · [U.S. Federal Acquisition Regulation on contract types](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/part-16)

## A launch contract and an internet bill

A satellite company or public agency can buy a **launch service** to get a satellite into orbit. NASA selected SpaceX to launch its StarBurst science satellite. Reuse allows hardware to fly again for another customer mission. The launch business still incurs operating costs and depreciation on flight hardware. [NASA’s StarBurst launch services award](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-awards-launch-services-for-starburst-gamma-ray-detector/) · [SpaceX Q3 2026 Form 10‑Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026066880/spcx-20260930.htm)

A Starlink customer pays for **internet access** and may buy a **kit with a terminal**. SpaceX’s Q3 2026 filing reports external launches in **Space**, and recurring connectivity service and kit sales in **Connectivity**. When it launches its own Starlink satellites, it capitalizes the launch costs in satellite assets and records no intersegment launch revenue. **SI** is another separately reported segment. [SpaceX Q3 2026 Form 10‑Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026066880/spcx-20260930.htm)

| Line to look for | What it helps explain |
| --- | --- |
| Engine equipment and services | Delivery of new engines, then parts and maintenance over the engines’ working lives |
| Government contracts and revenue | Value of orders awarded and work recorded as revenue in the period |
| Customer and internal launches | Launch services bought by customers and flights used to build the company’s own network |
| Starlink kits and connectivity services | Terminal sales and internet charges based on subscriptions or usage |
