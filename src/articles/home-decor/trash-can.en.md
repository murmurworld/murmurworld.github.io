What I wanted was a bin that would tie up a full bag for me. The Townew T Air X came pretty close. You press a button to heat-seal the top, lift the bag out, and the bin opens a fresh liner for you. [Townew T Air X product guide](https://townewonline.ca/products/t-air-x)

Then I wondered whether I could take that sealed bag straight outside. In Seoul, household trash goes out in a designated pay-as-you-throw bag, called a *jongnyangje* bag. I’d still have to put the sealed Townew liner inside one. [Seoul’s waste disposal guide](https://global.seoul.go.kr/hmpg/finf/elsi/wast/contPageDetail.do?conts_no=9B31E123320D4935BD07B1D413DF0035)

That was the appeal of the other bin on our list, the Daroom. A 20 L municipal bag fits straight into it. Wave a hand and the lid opens to either side. When the bag is full, you tie it yourself and it’s ready to go out.

## The bin in our home is a 27 L Magikan Hippo

Before we ordered the Townew, someone gave us a Magikan Hippo. That’s the one we use now. Stepping on the pedal opens both the outer and inner lids. When it’s time to empty it, we pull out as much liner as we need, cut it and tie it by hand. The Hippo uses its own liner, so we still need a municipal bag for collection. [Magikan Hippo guide](https://company.magikan.co.kr/contact/faq)

## The number to look for on the refill

For a Hippo marked 280, the refill needs to say 280 too. That was more useful to know than the bin’s capacity when I looked up prices. A five-pack of 280R5B rolls was about ₩17,000. Townew uses a refill ring instead. The manufacturer says one ring can seal up to 25 bags.
