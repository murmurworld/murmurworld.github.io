Of the five appliances we bundled together for our new place, the robot vacuum gave my fiancé the most pause. Here’s how he saw it.

> I went back and forth about the robot vacuum right up to the end. Did we really need one? We already had a vacuum. We’d saved money by ordering a bed frame from Ohouse, and the robot wouldn’t even fit underneath it. In the end we went with the subscription. It worked out cheaper for us.

The model we signed up for at the department store was the **Samsung Bespoke AI Steam Ultra (VR90F01AAG)**.

<h2 id="the-dock">The dock washes the mop pads</h2>

When the robot rolls back in, the dock empties its dustbin, washes the pads, then steams and dries them. The tanks are our job. We’ll need to fill one with clean water and empty the other after the pads have been washed.

Samsung also makes a version that connects to a water line and drain. The one we picked through Samsung AI Subscription has tanks. With the robot parked, it’s 44.4 cm wide, 52 cm tall and 51 cm deep. It needs a little extra room overhead to lift the tanks out. Our original shortlist assumed we’d have about 12 cm of clearance under the bed, which explains all the robots around 8 cm tall. Our Leel sofa also sits low to the floor. I liked that sofa enough to give up on having the robot clean underneath it.

## What we’ll check at setup

We’ll leave enough space for the robot to turn as it leaves the dock. On its first run I want to see how it handles the edge of the rug and the door thresholds. I’d better get the charging cables off the floor first.
