What I liked first was the shape of the seat. Each stitched square puffed up in the middle, and the arms sat low enough that I could see the shape from the side. When I sat down, I noticed how deep the seat was, too.

## Three other Monocus designs in photos

I looked at three other sofas from Monocus. In the brand’s photos, I could see just how differently they’d shaped the seats and arms.

## Why the sofa felt so soft

Of the four, the Leel was the one I could picture myself stretching out on. Sitting on it in the showroom made me think of our bed. I wondered if the sofa might actually be the softer of the two. The Monocus cutaway shows what’s inside the part you sit on. A blend of goose fill and fiber lies above and below a high-density foam core. The top layer gives when you sit down, and the foam underneath supports your weight.

## The pen mark that sold me on ivory

My biggest worry about ivory was how easily it might show dirt. In the showroom, the salesperson drew a line on the faux leather with a pen, then wiped it off right in front of us. Seeing the ink come off was what made me comfortable choosing ivory.

They told us it was a new material they also used on the Loyce, and that it held up well to cat and dog claws. The Leel comes in genuine leather too, but I liked the faux leather they’d shown me.

## Room to stretch out in 2.9 meters

We settled on **2.9 m across**. What I wanted was somewhere I could lie down and stretch my legs out. Monocus could make the two sections different depths, so we **extended the seat by the window** and kept the one by the side table short. I’d hoped to find a sofa the robot vacuum could get under, but the Leel sits too low for that.

<figure class="guide-photo">
  <img src="/media/monocus-sofa/leel-ivory-side.webp" width="537" height="401" alt="Official side view of an ivory Leel. The near seat is shorter and the one farther away extends into the room." loading="lazy" decoding="async">
  <figcaption>The two seats look much the same head-on. From the side, you can see how much farther one reaches. Styled photo from Monocus.</figcaption>

On the Monocus drawing, the straight three-seater is **290 cm wide, 104 cm deep and 65 cm tall**. The chaise version has the same width, but the long end is 160 cm deep. That’s **an extra 56 cm** to mark out on the floor toward the coffee table. These are the brand’s example dimensions.

It was the black Leel that stopped me in the showroom, and this is the ivory one we brought home. I’d heard that round shapes look good next to square ones. Now that I can see our round rug against those stitched squares, I’m glad we put the two together.

The owner of Monocus also runs a bed business, so we bought our bed there too.
